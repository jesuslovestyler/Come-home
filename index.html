Come Home

For the one who wandered — and wonders if they have gone too far.

You did not stop believing all at once. You drifted — a season of sorrow, a season of sin, a season of just being tired — and one day you looked up and God seemed very far away, and you were sure the distance was your fault and yours alone.

So you stayed away. Not out of rebellion, mostly. Out of shame. Shame whispers that the door you walked out of has been locked behind you, that too much time has passed, that you would need to become someone better before you could come back. It’s too late for me. I’ve gone too far.

This page exists because Jesus answered that very fear, out loud, in front of everyone. The tax collectors and sinners were drawing near to hear Him, and the respectable people murmured, “This man welcomes sinners, and eats with them.” Jesus did not argue. He told three stories — about a sheep, a coin, and a son. Each story is about something lost. Each one ends with joy. And each one is His answer to you.

◆

The lost sheep

Luke 15:4–7

Notice who does the searching. The sheep does not find its way home; the shepherd leaves the ninety-nine and goes out into the wilderness after the one. Before you ever turned toward God, He was already on the road toward you — and when He finds you, He does not drive you home with a stick. He carries you, rejoicing.

“Which of you men, if you had one hundred sheep, and lost one of them, wouldn’t leave the ninety-nine in the wilderness, and go after the one that was lost, until he found it? When he has found it, he carries it on his shoulders, rejoicing. When he comes home, he calls together his friends and his neighbors, saying to them, ‘Rejoice with me, for I have found my sheep which was lost!’ I tell you that even so there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents, than over ninety-nine righteous people who need no repentance.”

Luke 15:4–7

Heaven’s response to your return is not a sigh, not a probation period, not a disappointed lecture. It is more joy — joy greater than heaven feels over ninety-nine who never strayed. You are not the exception heaven tolerates. You are the occasion heaven celebrates.

◆

The lost coin

Luke 15:8–10

Maybe your wandering was quieter than the sheep’s. A coin does not run away; it slips from the hand and lies still in the dust, in the dark, in a corner of the house. Some people are lost like that — not in a far country, but right at home, unseen, feeling worthless and overlooked. Hear what the woman does: she lights a lamp, she sweeps the whole house, she seeks diligently — carefully, thoroughly — until she finds it. God searches the corners for you. One coin out of ten was worth lighting the lamp for.

“Or what woman, if she had ten drachma coins, if she lost one drachma coin, wouldn’t light a lamp, sweep the house, and seek diligently until she found it? When she has found it, she calls together her friends and neighbors, saying, ‘Rejoice with me, for I have found the drachma which I had lost.’ Even so, I tell you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner repenting.”

Luke 15:8–10

One sinner repenting. Not a crowd, not a multitude — one. If you are the one, the rejoicing of this story is yours.

The son, and the Father who runs

Luke 15:11–32

A man had two sons. The younger asked for his share of the inheritance early — in that world, almost like wishing his father dead — took it to a far country, and wasted everything. When the money was gone and a famine came, he ended up feeding pigs, hungry enough to envy them their husks, with no one giving him anything. There, at the bottom, he came to himself, and rehearsed a speech: he would go home, confess his sin, and ask only to be treated as a hired servant. It was a reasonable speech. It was even a humble one. He never got to finish it.

“He arose, and came to his father. But while he was still far off, his father saw him, and was moved with compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

Luke 15:20

The son managed only, “Father, I have sinned against heaven, and in your sight. I am no longer worthy to be called your son” — and the father did not answer with words at all. He answered with a robe, a ring, shoes, and a feast. Everything the son had forfeited, restored before the boy could finish apologizing for losing it.

“But the father said to his servants, ‘Bring out the best robe, and put it on him. Put a ring on his hand, and shoes on his feet. Bring the fattened calf, kill it, and let us eat, and celebrate; for this, my son, was dead, and is alive again. He was lost, and is found.’ They began to celebrate.”

Luke 15:22–24

Linger over one detail: while he was still far off, his father saw him. A man does not spot a lone figure at the far end of the road unless he is looking down that road. The father had been watching — day after day, through every season of the son’s absence — scanning the horizon for the shape of his child. He saw him far off because he had never stopped looking. And when he saw him, this dignified patriarch did the undignified thing: he gathered his robes and ran, taking the shame of the homecoming onto his own shoulders, so none of it would fall on his son. That is what God is like toward the one who turns home. He does not wait at the door with crossed arms. He is already on the road.

Even the elder brother, standing outside in the dark, angry at grace, was not left there. The father came out to him too, and pleaded with him — because there is more than one way to be far from home, and the Father goes out for both kinds of sons.

“But it was appropriate to celebrate and be glad, for this, your brother, was dead, and is alive again. He was lost, and is found.”

Luke 15:32

He will abundantly pardon

Isaiah 55:7

Centuries before Jesus told these stories, God had already said how He receives those who return. Not sparingly. Not barely. Abundantly.

“Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts; and let him return to Yahweh, and he will have mercy on him; and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon.”

Isaiah 55:7

So come. You do not need a better speech, a longer wait, or a cleaner record — the son brought none of those, and the father’s arms were open before a word of it mattered. Forsake the far country, turn your face toward home, and take the first step down the road. You will not walk it alone, and you will not walk all of it: somewhere along the way, there is running.

The running was for the road. Heaven is what the running was for.
